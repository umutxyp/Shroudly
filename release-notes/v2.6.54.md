## Shroudly 2.6.54

**When nothing else works: the Tor network.**

### Computers and Android
- When no technique opens a site (every one tried and nothing answered, or none of the site's addresses can be reached at all), Shroudly now sends that site through the **Tor network**. It reaches Tor through **Snowflake**: the connections of volunteers around the world, including everyone who turned on *Help people in censored countries* in Shroudly. Nobody's own address carries your traffic out; Tor's exits do, and no server of ours is involved.
- Only the sites that need it go through Tor; everything else goes as before. Tor starts only when a site first needs it and stops again after a quarter of an hour without use. If Snowflake does not get through, Tor's other ways (meek through a CDN, obfs4 bridges) are tried with it.
- Slower than a direct connection, and some sites ask Tor's visitors for a captcha. On by default; turn it off in **Settings → Connection → When nothing else works: the Tor network** (on Android: Settings, for the VPN and for Proxy only). Not used while **Your own server** is set, since that server already carries every site.
- Tor and its pluggable transports are the Tor Project's own builds (Tor Expert Bundle 16.0), checked against the hashes of their signed list.

### Snowflake, faster for everyone ([#73](https://github.com/umutxyp/Shroudly/issues/73))
- **Helping:** with *Help people in censored countries* on, a fast connection now takes more people at once, up to eight. It grows by one while every place is in use and nothing waits to go out, and gives one back as soon as something does. Measured on the traffic itself: no speed test, none of your data spent on one. A slow connection stays at one or two, as before.
- **The last way out:** Shroudly keeps one Snowflake volunteer ready beside the one in use, so when that volunteer goes away (a laptop closed, a browser tab shut) the connection moves on at once instead of waiting to be paired again. Only one: every volunteer held ready is a place someone else cannot have.
- What needs Tor's own broker (volunteers telling it their speed, people trying several and keeping the fastest) is proposed to the Snowflake team.

### Computers: where Shroudly keeps its data ([#72](https://github.com/umutxyp/Shroudly/issues/72))
- **Settings → Application → Data folder**: keep Shroudly's settings, logs and everything else it saves in a folder you choose (another drive, a synced folder), or **next to Shroudly** (portable). Everything is copied to the new place and Shroudly restarts; the old folder is left as it was.
- Portable without Settings: create a folder named `Shroudly Data` next to Shroudly.app (Mac), next to the AppImage (Linux), or next to Shroudly.exe in a copy you did not install (Windows). Not inside an installed Windows copy, because every update empties that folder.
- A chosen folder that is not there at a start (a USB stick not plugged in) is not waited for: Shroudly uses the default folder that time and says so in the log. What Shroudly changed on this computer (the system proxy as it was before) is always kept in the default folder, so it can always be put back.

### Mac App Store
- The same last way out on the Mac, inside the App Store app's network extension: Tor and Snowflake run there as libraries (Tor 0.4.9.15 from iCepa/Tor.framework, Snowflake and lyrebird from IPtProxy, the builds Orbot uses), checked against pinned hashes. Settings → When nothing else works: the Tor network.

### iPhone, iPad and Apple TV
- Not there: once ready, Tor and Snowflake take about 44 MB, and iOS ends a VPN extension at about 50 MB, which would end protection altogether. Apple TV has no build of Tor or Snowflake. *Help people in censored countries* keeps working on iPhone and iPad.

Everything from [2.6.53](https://github.com/umutxyp/Shroudly/releases/tag/v2.6.53) is included. Desktop users update automatically; the Android app from GitHub offers to install 2.6.54 itself.
