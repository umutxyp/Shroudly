## Shroudly 2.6.48

**See which address sites see through your own server, find your way around a tidier Settings, help people in censored countries from a computer or a server, and know what changed after each update.**

### Your own server: the address sites see ([#66](https://github.com/umutxyp/Shroudly/issues/66))
- While protection works through **Your own server**, the main window (desktop) and the main screen (Android, iPhone, iPad) say which address sites see, so you no longer have to open a "what is my IP" site to check that the server carries your connections. Testing a server in Settings says it too.
- It is asked of Cloudflare's trace page, through the server and the engine as every app's connection goes, a few seconds after protection starts and then every ten minutes; the site's certificate is checked. The address is shown, never logged (logs are often attached to public issues) and never sent anywhere.

### Settings in four tabs (desktop)
- **General** (the language, testing a site, Advanced mode, the modes for Discord, games and Store apps), **Connection** (the technique, encrypted DNS and your own DNS server, IPv6, which sites go through Shroudly, ads and trackers), **Server and sharing** (your own server, sharing your connection, helping people in censored countries) and **Application** (starting with the computer, the tray, notifications, updates, Repair, Reset, Quit, the colours). The long page and its "More settings" fold are gone.

### Help people in censored countries
- **Said plainly.** The section now says what it does in Shroudly's own words: people where the internet is censored use a little of your connection to get past the blocks; they cannot reach your computer or network, what they open stays encrypted, the sites they open never see your address, and nothing is recorded. How it works (Tor's Snowflake) is one line under it.
- **Asked now and then.** While protection works, the main window (and on Android, iPhone and iPad, the main screen, with Psiphon's Conduit) asks once whether you would like to help, first after three days of use. Each "Not now" doubles the wait: a week, two weeks, four… An update keeps where it was. It is never asked where Tor itself is blocked (Iran, Russia, China, Turkmenistan, Belarus, Myanmar), where the user is among the people who need the help.
- **From a server, with no screen.** `shroudly --help-others` runs only the help: no window, no protection, nothing of the system touched. One line sets it up on a Linux server as a service, as a user of its own, updated weekly and removable in one command ([On a server](https://github.com/umutxyp/Shroudly#on-a-server)). Like on a desktop, the people helped reach only Tor's relays through it, and the sites they open see a Tor exit's address, never the server's.

### iPhone and iPad ([#65](https://github.com/umutxyp/Shroudly/issues/65))
- A copy signed with a **free Apple ID** (AltStore, SideStore, Sideloadly…) loses Apple's VPN permission, and iOS then never asks for it. Shroudly used to say it "needs the VPN permission"; it now says what happened and links to how to install it properly: **TrollStore** (iOS 16.0–16.6.1, 16.7 RC, 17.0) or a **paid** Apple developer account. The App Store copy follows once the developer account is approved.

### After each update
- Once, a short note in your language says what changed: a card on the desktop's Protection panel, a message on Android, iPhone and iPad. Never after a first install.

### Also
- Tests: the four tabs, the address sites see (through a real server, certificate checked), the card asking for help and its doubling waits, and what changed after an update, in the real app on every OS; `--help-others` with no display helping a person over real WebRTC on every OS, started from every Linux package and both AppImages, and the whole server set-up run on a real Ubuntu machine with systemd before the release was published.
- New text in all 31 languages.

Everything from [2.6.47](https://github.com/umutxyp/Shroudly/releases/tag/v2.6.47) is included. Desktop users update automatically; the Android app from GitHub offers to install 2.6.48 itself.
